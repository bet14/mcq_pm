🎯 Core Concepts

What is Earned Value Analysis?

Earned Value Analysis (EVA) is a project management technique that integrates scope, schedule, and resources to assess project performance objectively. It measures project progress from two critical perspectives:

  • Time (Schedule): Is the project ahead or behind schedule?
  • Cost (Budget): Is the project under or over budget?

The Three Foundation Metrics

1️⃣ Planned Value (PV)

Also known as: Budgeted Cost of Work Scheduled (BCWS)

PV = Planned % Complete × Task Budget

Meaning: The budgeted amount of work that should have been completed by now.

Example: If a task should be 50% done and has a $10,000 budget, PV = $5,000

2️⃣ Earned Value (EV)

Also known as: Budgeted Cost of Work Performed (BCWP)

EV = Actual % Complete × Task Budget

Meaning: The budgeted value of work that has actually been completed.

Example: If work is actually 60% done and budget is $10,000, EV = $6,000

3️⃣ Actual Cost (AC)

Also known as: Actual Cost of Work Performed (ACWP)

AC = Actual Spending to Date

Meaning: The real money spent on the work completed so far.

Example: If you've actually spent $7,500, then AC = $7,500

Key Insight: The Relationship

These three metrics create a powerful triangle:

  • PV vs EV: Tells you about schedule performance
  • EV vs AC: Tells you about cost performance
  • All three together: Give complete project health picture

📐 Formulas & Calculations

Derived Metrics (Current Status)

Schedule Variance (SV)

SV = EV - PV

Interpretation:

✓ SV > 0 = Ahead of Schedule

= SV = 0 = On Schedule

✗ SV < 0 = Behind Schedule

Schedule Performance Index (SPI)

SPI = EV / PV

Interpretation:

✓ SPI > 1 = Ahead of Schedule

= SPI = 1 = On Schedule

✗ SPI < 1 = Behind Schedule

Example: SPI = 1.25 means 25% ahead of schedule

Cost Variance (CV)

CV = EV - AC

Interpretation:

✓ CV > 0 = Under Budget

= CV = 0 = On Budget

✗ CV < 0 = Over Budget

Cost Performance Index (CPI)

CPI = EV / AC

Interpretation:

✓ CPI > 1 = Under Budget

= CPI = 1 = On Budget

✗ CPI < 1 = Over Budget

Example: CPI = 0.85 means 15% over budget

Forecast Metrics (Future Projections)

Budget at Completion (BAC)

BAC = Total Project Budget

Meaning: The overall approved budget for the entire project.

Estimate at Completion (EAC)

Three calculation methods depending on assumptions:

Method 1: EAC = BAC / CPI

Use when: Current variances will continue into the future

Method 2: EAC = AC + (BAC - EV)

Use when: Current variances are atypical and won't continue

Method 3: EAC = AC + [(BAC - EV) / (SPI × CPI)]

Use when: Both schedule and cost performance affect future

Estimate to Complete (ETC)

ETC = EAC - AC

Meaning: How much more money must be spent to finish the project.

Variance at Completion (VAC)

VAC = BAC - EAC

Interpretation:

✓ VAC > 0 = Surplus (under budget at completion)

✗ VAC < 0 = Deficit (over budget at completion)

To Complete Performance Index (TCPI)

TCPI = (BAC - EV) / (BAC - AC)

Meaning: What CPI is needed for remaining work to finish on budget.

✗ TCPI > 1 = Must improve efficiency to stay on budget

📈 Understanding Each Metric

Schedule Metrics vs Cost Metrics

⏱️ Schedule Metrics

SV (Schedule Variance)

Dollar amount ahead/behind schedule

SPI (Schedule Performance Index)

Percentage efficiency of schedule

💰 Cost Metrics

CV (Cost Variance)

Dollar amount over/under budget

CPI (Cost Performance Index)

Percentage efficiency of spending

Absolute vs Relative Metrics

Metric Type Absolute (Variance) Relative (Index)
Schedule SV (in dollars) SPI (as ratio)
Cost CV (in dollars) CPI (as ratio)
Use Case Understand magnitude of variance Compare across different projects
Example $5,000 over budget 0.95 CPI (5% over budget)

Why Both Metrics Matter

Scenario: A $1,000,000 project has CV = -$50,000

  • Absolute view: "We're $50K over budget" (sounds bad)
  • Relative view: CPI = 0.95 (only 5% over - manageable)

Scenario: A $100,000 project has CV = -$50,000

  • Absolute view: "We're $50K over budget" (same dollar amount)
  • Relative view: CPI = 0.67 (33% over - critical!)

Index Interpretation Quick Reference

Index Value Status Meaning
> 1.0 ✓ Good Ahead of schedule or under budget
= 1.0 = Neutral On schedule or on budget
< 1.0 ✗ Bad Behind schedule or over budget

🔄 Comparison & Relationships

Schedule Variance vs Cost Variance

Aspect Schedule Variance (SV) Cost Variance (CV)
Formula SV = EV - PV CV = EV - AC
Measures Work progress vs planned schedule Work value vs actual spending
Positive Result Ahead of schedule Under budget
Negative Result Behind schedule Over budget
Independence A project can be ahead of schedule but over budget (or vice versa)

Four Project Scenarios

✓✓ Best Case: Under Budget & Ahead of Schedule

Conditions: SV > 0, CV > 0, SPI > 1, CPI > 1

Project is performing excellently on all fronts. Likely to finish early and under budget.

⚠️ Warning: Over Budget but Ahead of Schedule

Conditions: SV > 0, CV < 0, SPI > 1, CPI < 1

Work is progressing quickly, but too much money is being spent. May finish on time but over budget.

⚠️ Warning: Under Budget but Behind Schedule

Conditions: SV < 0, CV > 0, SPI < 1, CPI > 1

Spending is controlled, but work is progressing slowly. May finish under budget but late.

✗✗ Worst Case: Over Budget & Behind Schedule

Conditions: SV < 0, CV < 0, SPI < 1, CPI < 1

Project is in trouble on both fronts. Likely to finish late and over budget.

Variance vs Index: When to Use Each

Situation Use Variance (SV/CV) Use Index (SPI/CPI)
Single project analysis ✓ Good for understanding exact dollar amounts ✓ Good for understanding efficiency %
Comparing multiple projects ✗ Not useful (different budgets) ✓ Essential (normalized percentages)
Stakeholder reporting ✓ Executives understand dollars ✓ Project managers understand ratios
Trend analysis Shows absolute trend Shows relative trend (better)

The Three Foundation Metrics Relationship

PV (Planned) vs EV (Earned) vs AC (Actual)

PV = What we planned to spend EV = What work is worth AC = What we actually spent

  • If EV > PV: More work done than planned (ahead)
  • If EV < PV: Less work done than planned (behind)
  • If EV > AC: Getting good value for money (under budget)
  • If EV < AC: Paying too much for work done (over budget)

💼 Real-World Use Cases

Case Study 1: Software Development Project

Printer Driver Development

Project Scope: Develop and install 10 printer drivers

Budget: $100,000 ($10K per driver)

Timeline: 10 weeks (1 driver per week)

At Week 5:

  • 4 drivers completed (should have 5)
  • Spent $47,500 (should have spent $50,000)

EVA Analysis:

  • PV = $50,000 (5 drivers × $10K)
  • EV = $40,000 (4 drivers × $10K)
  • AC = $47,500 (actual spending)
  • SV = -$10,000 (behind schedule)
  • CV = -$7,500 (over budget)
  • SPI = 0.80 (20% behind schedule)
  • CPI = 0.84 (16% over budget)

Action: Project is in trouble on both fronts. Need to investigate delays and cost overruns.

Case Study 2: Construction Project

Building Renovation:

A $500,000 office renovation project is 60% complete after 3 months (planned 50%).

Actual spending: $280,000 (planned: $250,000)

EVA Results:

  • PV = $250,000
  • EV = $300,000 (60% × $500K)
  • AC = $280,000
  • SV = +$50,000 ✓ (ahead of schedule)
  • CV = +$20,000 ✓ (under budget)

Insight: Excellent project - ahead and under budget. Likely to finish early and save money.

Case Study 3: Marketing Campaign

Digital Marketing Initiative:

A $50,000 campaign should be 40% complete but is only 30% complete.

Spending is on track at $15,000.

EVA Results:

  • PV = $20,000 (40% × $50K)
  • EV = $15,000 (30% × $50K)
  • AC = $15,000
  • SV = -$5,000 ✗ (behind schedule)
  • CV = $0 ✓ (on budget)

Insight: Campaign is delayed but spending is controlled. Need to accelerate activities without increasing budget.

When to Use EVA

✓ EVA Works Best For:

  • Large, complex projects with significant budgets
  • Long-duration projects where tracking is critical
  • Projects with multiple phases and deliverables
  • When both schedule and cost control are important
  • Comparing multiple projects' performance
  • Forecasting project completion and final costs

When EVA May Be Overkill

⚠️ Consider Alternatives For:

  • Very small projects (< $50K)
  • Short-duration projects (< 1 month)
  • Simple projects with clear deliverables
  • Projects where only schedule matters
  • Agile/iterative projects (use different metrics)
